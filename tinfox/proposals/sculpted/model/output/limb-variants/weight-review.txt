TINFOX MASS REVIEW

printed: custom limbs 2.381 kg; robot base 12.092 kg; relief savings 0.000 kg
hybrid: custom limbs 3.287 kg; robot base 12.989 kg; relief savings 0.065 kg
cnc: custom limbs 4.374 kg; robot base 14.077 kg; relief savings 0.742 kg

The initial metal route retained printed-part dimensions. At assumed solid densities,
aluminum weighs 2.13 times as much as PETG for identical geometry. The solid steel
pushrods also add about 0.226 kg across four legs. Material substitution alone does
not exploit metal stiffness or strength.

The first relief pass removes material behind the thigh motor walls and collar,
from the inside of the motor face, from the hip hub and from an accessible annular
pocket in each roll carrier. Nonstructural metal shoulder covers have thinner walls.
The printed prototype geometry is retained. No finite-element optimization or
physical strength test supports further reductions yet.

Twelve motors: 4.86 kg. Assumed batteries: 1.40 kg. Electronics/wiring allowance:
0.80 kg. Additional unmodeled mounting/hardware allowance: 0.40 kg. These total
7.46 kg before the modeled frame, custom limbs, bearings, hardware and feet.
Battery masses and actual peripheral packages remain assumptions.

The remaining large opportunities are the paired thigh structures, motor-adapter
architecture, hardware stacks and nonstructural covers. Reducing those requires
stiffness, fatigue, bearing alignment, clamp preload and CNC fixture analysis.
The steel pushrod should be replaced only after buckling and joint-eye fatigue
are checked. Current metal designs remain preliminary and above the 10 kg goal.
